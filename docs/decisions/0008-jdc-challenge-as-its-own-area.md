# ADR 0008 — The JDC Challenge gets its own area, and the belt follows your best

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 20 September 2026
**Amends:** ADR 0007 (decision 3)

---

## Context

ADR 0007 made the JDC Challenge the Foundation programme's fortnightly
assessment and nothing else: `trainingOnly: true`, reachable only as the
eighth session in the queue, its history a flat list of grade/score/date rows
on `/training`. The seed shipped no attempts, so that list had never rendered
with data in it.

Reviewing it turned up a scoring bug underneath all of that. The engine
awarded a Shanghai 100 points *instead of* the three darts' face value, where
the real rule adds 100 *on top*. A Shanghai on the 15 scored 100 while three
treble 15s scored 135 — the engine penalised the best outcome in the round,
and every recorded grade sat on a scale no other JDC implementation uses.

Tom's framing: the point is "to track progress against the proper challenge
and belt/score awards". Both halves of that matter — *proper* rules the rest
of the world would recognise, and the belt awards as the thing you climb.

## Decision

1. **The scoring is corrected to the published rule.** The Shanghai bonus is
   additive. History is backfilled rather than reset: the dart log is
   authoritative (ADR 0003), so every past attempt re-scores exactly, and
   `scripts/backfill-jdc.ts` repairs the cached projection that `/history`
   and the practice personal bests read.

2. **The challenge gets its own area at `/jdc`,** reachable from `/training`
   and `/practice`, with a start button. It stays out of the practice drill
   grid: it is a test, not a drill, and spec 0005's eight-game scope rule
   stands. It also stays the programme's fortnightly assessment — ADR 0007's
   cadence is unchanged, it simply is no longer the only way in.

3. **The belt follows the best attempt ever thrown, not the latest.** A grade
   is something you reached; one bad night does not take it back off you. The
   trend chart carries every attempt, so nothing is hidden — the belt is an
   award, the trend is the honesty.

4. **Ad-hoc attempts count exactly like assessments.** They set the belt and
   appear in the trend. Programme assessments are marked with a tick so the
   fortnightly cadence stays readable, but throwing the test twice in a week
   is a legitimate thing to do and hiding it would be worse than labelling it.

5. **The belt ladder is the screen's spine.** Seven cells, earned ones filled
   in the JDC's own colour and dated, unearned ones showing their threshold,
   so the ladder doubles as the target list. Belts are 150 points apart, so
   the per-part breakdown against per-part bests carries the progress between
   jumps.

## Consequences

- The seven belt colours live in `lib/jdc/belts.ts`, deliberately outside the
  Tailwind palette: they are the JDC's award shirts, not an Arrows device, and
  nobody should restyle them to fit the app.
- No new tables. `jdcReport(darts)` and `buildJdcSummary(attempts)` are pure,
  and `/jdc` derives everything on read — the third time ADR 0003 has paid for
  itself, and the reason the scoring fix repaired history for free.
- `/api/training`'s `assessments` array now means "every completed JDC
  attempt", ad-hoc ones included. `/training` no longer lists them; it shows
  the belt and links to `/jdc`.
- The seed ships five attempts (282 → 520, crossing Purple, Yellow and Green),
  generated by throwing at the real targets through the bot's scatter model.
  The programme's own session rows are **not** seeded, so a fresh seed still
  starts the Foundation queue empty.
- Part 2 throws one dart at every double, so the seed no longer leaves any
  double untried. The doubles heatmap gained real coverage as a side effect,
  and `seed-validation` now asserts cold doubles (tried, never hit) where it
  asserted untried ones.
- Deferred: the per-attempt detail screen. The hub answers what is worth
  asking between attempts, and part 2's misses already reach the doubles
  heatmap. Build it when the heatmap proves not to be enough.
